Classify each of the {n} ICA components shown in this grid (labeled {labels}).

Each component shows:
- Topography map (scalp distribution)
- Time series (first 2.5 seconds)
- ERP-style image (continuous data segments)
- Power spectrum (1-55Hz)

Weigh converging evidence across all four panels for each component; do not rely on one panel alone.

Valid labels and distinguishing cues:
- "brain": Scalp map is roughly dipolar and not eye/edge focused, often central/parietal/temporal.
  Spectrum usually falls with frequency (1/f-like) and may show peaks around 5-30 Hz, especially
  alpha near 10 Hz. Time series is usually smoother/rhythmic rather than blink-like, step-like,
  or high-frequency bursting.
- "eye": Scalp map is frontal/periocular. Blinks often show strong bilateral frontal activity,
  large slow spikes/deflections, and power concentrated below about 5 Hz. Horizontal eye movement
  often shows left-right frontal polarity and step-like activity with stable intervals separated
  by fast saccade transitions. When the scalp map is frontal/periocular AND the time series or
  spectrum shows low-frequency-dominant, blink- or saccade-like structure, prefer eye over a
  generic dipolar brain read or a single-electrode channel_noise read.
- "muscle": Power spectrum is the leading clue: broad high-frequency power, often above 20 Hz,
  with a flat or rising high-frequency profile rather than clean 1/f decay. Scalp map may look
  shallow, edge-focused, temporal/jaw/frontal, or locally dipolar near muscle areas -- including
  a topography that looks like it is dominated by one or two nearby electrodes. Activity may
  appear spiky, erratic, or bursty. Any high-frequency spectral elevation (flat or rising power
  above roughly 20-30 Hz) points to muscle, not channel_noise, even when the scalp map also looks
  locally focal -- topography alone does not override spectral evidence here.
- "heart": Look for regular ECG/QRS-like deflections about once per second in the time series or
  repeated bands in the segment image. Scalp map is often a broad near-linear gradient from a far
  source. A clear, regular ~1 Hz QRS-like rhythm is decisive for heart on its own, even if the
  scalp map is broad, diffuse, or non-dipolar.
- "line_noise": A sharp narrow peak at 50 or 60 Hz is decisive when that frequency is visible.
  Do not call line_noise for a notch/dip at 50/60 Hz or for mild line noise mixed into another
  clearer source.
- "channel_noise": This is a narrow, high-bar category, not a default guess for a noisy- or
  spiky-looking component. Both conditions must hold: (1) the scalp map is unambiguously
  dominated by a single isolated electrode -- a sharp one-channel "island" with no smooth spatial
  falloff to neighboring sites and no opposite pole, AND (2) the power spectrum is flat or noisy
  WITHOUT the rising/elevated high-frequency profile described under muscle. If the spectrum shows
  that muscle-like high-frequency elevation, classify as muscle even if the topography looks
  focal to one electrode. If evidence is ambiguous between an isolated bad electrode and a diffuse
  or unclear source, prefer muscle or other_artifact over channel_noise.
- "other_artifact": Use when evidence is mixed, non-dipolar, splotchy/multipolar, contradictory,
  or does not clearly fit the categories above. This is a common, correct answer -- roughly one
  in six components in typical EEG data genuinely belongs here -- not a last resort to avoid.
  When panels genuinely conflict, the visual evidence is weak, or no category fits cleanly,
  other_artifact is the correct label: do not force channel_noise, muscle, eye, or a weak brain
  read onto an ambiguous component just to give a more specific-sounding answer.

Confidence guidance:
- Use high confidence only when multiple panels agree or one decisive cue is present
  (clear blink/saccade, clear QRS rhythm, sharp 50/60 Hz peak, or isolated bad channel with a
  clean, non-elevated spectrum).
- Use lower confidence when cues conflict, the source looks mixed, or the component could
  plausibly be confused with another class.
