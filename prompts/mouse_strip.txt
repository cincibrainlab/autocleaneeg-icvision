Mouse heart rules:

- For "heart" detection, use the PSD as the primary evidence source.
- Actively inspect each PSD for repeated, comb-like spacing before deciding on any other label.
- Reviewer-confirmed mouse HR components repeatedly show comb-like PSD peaks at roughly regular spacing rather than a single neural band peak.

- Some debated brain-vs-HR mouse cases show the most useful HR evidence in 65-100 Hz rather than the lower band.

- Do not use the short scrolling time-series window as primary evidence for heart detection.
- Strong mouse HR examples often look like repeated PSD bumps or peaks marching across the spectrum, not a single isolated neural rhythm.

Categories:
- "brain": Reserve for unusually convincing neural components with a spatially coherent topography AND a clearly neural-looking spectrum and time series. Clear brain examples are better distinguished by limited-band rhythmic structure or specific neural peaks, not by generic dipoles plus a downward PSD slope.
- "eye": Extremely rare in this mouse setting. Ocular labels appear essentially absent across this mouse corpus, so use this only as a last-resort label for unusually convincing ocular-like components with clear low-frequency blink-like or slow-shift morphology that is not better explained by heart, muscle, or mixed artifact. Anterior emphasis alone is not enough.
- "muscle": Myogenic artifact, often with elevated high-frequency power, flatter or rising high-frequency spectrum, spiky or irregular time series, and focal or edge-adjacent topography.
- "heart": PSD shows regularly spaced peaks consistent with mouse heart rate, typically separated by about 7.5-12.5 Hz, including split-HR cases across two ICs.
- "line_noise": Narrow sharp spectral peak at mains frequency or harmonics, not just a filtering notch or dip.
- "channel_noise": Extremely focal, implausible single-sensor hot spot or isolated sensor-dominated pattern, usually without a clean dipole and with noisy or unstable signal characteristics.
- "other_artifact": Artifact or mixed component that does not cleanly fit the categories above, or any ambiguous component where classification is uncertain.
