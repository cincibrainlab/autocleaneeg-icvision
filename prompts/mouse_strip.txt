Categories:
- "brain": Reserve for unusually convincing neural components with a spatially coherent topography AND a clearly neural-looking spectrum and time series. A rough dipole or a generic 1/f falloff alone is not enough in mouse data.
- "eye": Rare in this mouse setting. Reserve for unusually convincing ocular-like components that are clearly rostral/anterior AND dominated by very low frequencies with repeated slow deflections, step-like shifts, or blink-like transients. Anterior emphasis alone is not enough.
- "muscle": Myogenic artifact, often with elevated high-frequency power, flatter or rising high-frequency spectrum, spiky or irregular time series, and focal or edge-adjacent topography.
- "heart": PSD shows regularly spaced peaks consistent with mouse heart rate, typically separated by about 7.5-12.5 Hz, including split-HR cases across two ICs.
- "line_noise": Narrow sharp spectral peak at mains frequency or harmonics, not just a filtering notch or dip.
- "channel_noise": Extremely focal, implausible single-sensor hot spot or isolated sensor-dominated pattern, usually without a clean dipole and with noisy or unstable signal characteristics.
- "other_artifact": Artifact or mixed component that does not cleanly fit the categories above, or any ambiguous component where classification is uncertain.

Decision rules:
- Do not require human-style frontal eye dipoles to call "eye", but still require strong positive ocular evidence.
- Never call "eye" from weak anteriority, broad low-frequency drift, or map ambiguity alone.
- If regularly spaced heart-rate PSD peaks are present, prefer "heart" over "eye".
- If regularly spaced heart-rate PSD peaks are present, prefer "heart" over "brain".
- If regularly spaced heart-rate PSD peaks are present, prefer "heart" over "other_artifact".

- If elevated high-frequency power, spiky activity, or edge-adjacent/focal contamination is present, prefer "muscle" over "eye".
- If the component looks artifactual but the case for "eye" is weak, mixed, or indirect, use "other_artifact" instead.
- Do not call "brain" just because the map looks roughly dipolar or the PSD slopes downward.
- Favor "brain" only when all three views support it together: coherent topography, convincingly neural time series, and a clearly neural PSD without strong competing artifact evidence.
- If heart-rate spacing, focal contamination, spiky activity, diffuse noise, or any clear artifact cue is present, do not call "brain".
- If uncertain between "brain" and any artifact class, prefer the artifact class or "other_artifact", not "brain".
- Use "other_artifact" as a fallback for unclear artifacts, not as a substitute for plausible "heart".
